## Abstract

In 1999 I started a software company called **Protagona** — from *protagonist*, the person at the
center of the drama. The name was the thesis: software should be built around the person using
it, not the process it automates. Joseph Pine had just published *The Experience Economy*, I
hired him as a consultant, we partnered with Macromedia, and I built a framework called
Experience Management to turn his ideas into a product. It took `[TEAM SIZE]` people and
`[ELAPSED TIME]`.

We shipped. But we couldn't build the thing the name promised, because the tools didn't exist.

This year Pine published *The Transformation Economy*. I'm translating it into software again —
this time alone, in `[ELAPSED TIME]`, for two very different products: one for travelers, and one
for families caring for someone with dementia. The same engine runs both. A single sentence —
*"he won't eat"* — produces three different and equally correct answers depending on who is
asking and where they are in the illness.

So this is a before-and-after with an unusually long baseline: the same author, the same kind of
translation problem, twenty-seven years apart, and one person who did it both times.

**The conclusion is not that everything got easier.** The thinking didn't get faster at all — the
ideas still took years, and Pine's took decades. What collapsed is implementation. In 1999 I
needed a team because turning an idea into software was the expensive part. In 2026 the expensive
part is knowing which idea is worth turning into software, and that still takes someone who has
lived the problem.

And something new got harder. In 1999 software could be wrong, but it couldn't be *confidently*
wrong. I'll show three failures from the last month that were impossible a quarter century ago —
including a knowledge base in which 94% of the citations pointed back at the system itself.

## What you'll learn

- Why translating a concept into a product is a different skill from having the concept
- What a 27-year before-and-after actually shows about AI and the cost of building
- Why personalization makes a system's errors *more* convincing, not less — and what to do about it

## Speaker

Michael Emerson is the founder of Three Realms Travel and Meet My CAL. He founded Protagona in
1999, where he worked with Joseph Pine to translate *The Experience Economy* into software, and
co-founded Hunhu Healthcare, winner of AARP's Innovation Award at the 2015 Minnesota Cup. He has
spoken at more than 100 corporate events worldwide.
